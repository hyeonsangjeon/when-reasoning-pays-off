Multi-step work: when reasoning earns its trial

The second benchmark is the counterweight to the short-factual trap. Here, the task asks for more than recall. It asks the model to keep a small chain of constraints alive long enough to answer correctly. In that setting, reasoning effort has something useful to do.

Question

When does extra reasoning buy a quality improvement instead of only a larger bill?

The quality movement changed the interpretation

In benchmark-02, the baseline GPT-4o cell averaged a judge score of 1.5. GPT-5.2 at none, medium, high, and extra-high effort reached 2.0, and low effort reached 1.933333. The chart does not say “always raise effort.” It says the workload has enough structure for reasoning to matter.

The more important reading is the shape of the first successful cells. GPT-5.2 none averaged $0.000618 per request and reached 2.0. That makes the trial useful without turning the highest effort setting into the default.

How to read the chart

The X-axis is the model/effort cell. The Y-axis is mean judge score. The label below each bar is mean cost per request for the same cell. This paired view matters because a quality bar alone can tempt over-selection: if several cells reach the top of the rubric, the cheaper and faster one becomes more interesting.

This is not a frequency histogram. Each bar is a summarized experimental cell with its own sample count and standard deviation in the source table.

Why this benchmark was different

The short-factual task mostly asked for brief answers. The multi-step task asked the model to preserve a small reasoning chain. That changed what “extra thinking” could buy. The reasoning budget was no longer just hidden spend; it was a path to fewer wrong answers.

Still, the evidence does not reward maximalism. Latency rises as effort rises, and extra-high effort averaged 3.3s. Once quality has reached the top of the rubric, the next question becomes whether a slower cell is saying anything new.

The story to keep

Reasoning pays when it changes the answer, not when it changes the aura of the answer. Multi-step work is where the trial belongs first: the task has enough internal structure for the model to benefit, and the evaluator can see the difference.

The decision is therefore not “use more reasoning everywhere.” It is “route multi-step slices to the lowest effort level that clears the quality bar.”
