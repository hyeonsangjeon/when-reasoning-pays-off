Invisible reasoning tokens: the part of the bill the answer does not show

The short-factual run looked ordinary if you only read the answers. Most responses were brief. The bill told a different story because reasoning models can spend tokens inside the model before producing the visible text. Those internal tokens are not shown to the user, but they are part of the measured usage surface.

Question

If the answer text stays short, where did the extra cost come from?

The answer was short; the internal work was not

The useful surprise in benchmark-01 is not that extra-high effort cost more. That part is expected. The useful surprise is how little of the increase was visible in the final answer. Minimal effort averaged 12.7 visible output tokens and 3.7 reasoning tokens. Extra-high effort averaged 17.9 visible output tokens and 311.5 reasoning tokens.

That means the text a reader sees is a poor proxy for the computation that was purchased. A short answer can hide a large reasoning budget, and a plausible answer can still be a bad economic trade if the paired quality chart does not move.

How to read the chart

The bars are aggregate effort cells, not a histogram of individual requests. The X-axis is the reasoning-effort setting. The Y-axis is mean reasoning tokens per request. The small label under each bar gives mean visible output tokens for the same cell.

Read the chart from bottom to top: first ask whether visible output changed enough to explain the bill; then compare the hidden reasoning bar. In this slice, the explanation is not answer length. It is internal work.

What the source charts add

Token composition explains the mechanism, but it is not enough by itself. The cost chart shows the bill growing. The quality chart asks whether that bill bought a better answer. The latency chart shows the user-facing wait. Together they make the hidden surface legible: more internal tokens, more spend, more latency, and no matching quality lift in this short factual slice.

The story to keep

The lesson is not that reasoning tokens are bad. They are the point of a reasoning model. The lesson is that they need a job. If the task only needs a short factual answer, internal reasoning can become a silent budget leak. If the task needs multi-step resolution, those same tokens may be exactly what earns the quality gain.

This is why the overview essay keeps cost, quality, latency, and token composition together. The bill is not fully visible in the answer.
